\noindent\textbf{This document does not claim a theorem for every \(k\).}

The conjecture asks whether for every \(k\ge 1\) there exist integers
\(N_1<\cdots<N_k\) with \(\lvert\bigcap_i D(N_i)\rvert\ge k\), where
\(D(n)=\{\lvert a-b\rvert:n=ab\}\).

Cases \(k=2,3,4\) are in the literature. This workspace reconstructs
their mechanisms, proves auxiliary lemmas, and records an adversarial
audit. It does not prove the remaining cases and does not disprove them.

\paragraph{Question 1.}
A complete proof must differ from Erdős--Rosenfeld (two differences),
Jiménez-Urroz (three differences via a positive-rank elliptic curve),
Bremner (a special locus for four differences), and Choudhry's
unrestricted additive square tables (the square-side condition is
exactly 885). None of those texts supplies an argument for arbitrary
\(k\).

\paragraph{Question 2.}
The naive count \(2-(k-2)^2<0\) for \(k\ge 4\) explains failed search.
The geometric obstruction of the fixed-difference method is the genus
\(g=1+2^{m-2}(m-3)\). Crossing that obstruction for every \(k\) requires
an identity, a lift \(k\to k+1\), or a local-to-global theorem that is
not presently known. Local solubility holds for every \(k\) and is not
a disproof.

\texttt{lemmas/missing\_for\_all\_k.md} for the remaining targets.
